Design options

Fronts first, then backs. Same three Pokémon on every front so you can compare typefaces and layouts. Scene photos are type-matched (sun, surf, fog, lightning, aurora, nebula). Backs must look the same on every card or the pile leaks which Pokémon is coming — except B5, which is a collectible puzzle, not a play back. Reply with a front number (1–6) and a back (B1–B8).

Front of the card

1 Cinematic poster

Sports-movie energy. Full-bleed photo, oversized sprite, giant Bebas Neue name, Oswald film-strip stats. Dark gradient keeps numbers readable. Most “exciting” at arm’s length.

2 Classic Top Trumps

Winning Moves yellow chevrons, cream stock, gold art window — but the window is a real photo instead of a white box. Name in Anton. The safest “this is still Top Trumps” option.

3 Comic smash

Bangers type, halftone dots, starburst, fat black borders, two-column smash numbers. Loud on the table; stats are the easiest to read of the photo treatments.

4 Pokédex terminal

Share Tech Mono, scanlines, dotted leaders, green-on-black HUD. Photo sits as a ghosted backdrop. Reads as a device, not a toy card.

5 Field dossier

Manila folder, Polaroid of the scene, Barlow Condensed ruled stats, red TYPE stamp. Scout-report energy — good if the game should feel like a field guide rather than a poster.

6 Stadium jersey

Black Ops One nameplate, giant faded card number, type-coloured banner, compact stat grid. Trading-card / FIFA-sticker energy. Photo still full-bleed.

Back of the card

On a real Winning Moves pack the reverse is the same on every play card, so you cannot read the pile. It is usually the yellow stacked TOP / TRUMPS wordmark (thick black outline, oversized O, condensed TRUMPS, tagline “Collectable, Competitive, Compulsive!”) plus the pack’s title or a single piece of key art. Some packs print a 30-piece poster puzzle instead. Extra cards (rules, barcode) get different backs; the play deck does not.

Our drawing of that lockup — not Winning Moves’ trademark file. Yellow fill, black outline, chunky TOP, condensed TRUMPS, blue field. B1 uses this plate; B7–B8 put it on pack art and a plastic case.

B1 Classic wordmark

Closest to a Winning Moves pack: the yellow/blue lockup as the whole reverse, pack name under it. Safest “this is Top Trumps” back.

B2 Pokéball

Red / white split, black belt, centre button. Reads from across a table and in a digital pile. CSS wordmark on the white half.

B3 Playing-card pattern

Bicycle-deck energy: repeating dots, double gold ring, wordmark in a medallion. Hides wear and looks like a proper reverse.

B4 Table felt

Green baize like the gallery background, cream diamond, wordmark as a chip. Pairs with a pub-table / sports-card mood.

B5 Puzzle poster

A real Winning Moves trick: each reverse is one tile of a poster (here a nebula, 5×8 = 40 cards). Fun to collect — do not use as the play back, because a trained eye could learn tiles. Left is the assembled poster; the two cards are sample tiles.

B6 Holo foil

Dark space photo, violet rim, moving sheen. Matches cinematic / stadium fronts. Still identical on every card.

B7 Pack key art

How licensed packs usually look: one illustration for the whole deck (Poké Ball in fire, water, lightning, aurora) with the wordmark stuck on like a title plate. Same picture on all 40 cards.

B8 Plastic case

The other thing people remember: the hard red travel case. Glossy moulded lid, gold pinstripe, wordmark in the recessed label. Feels like the object you pull out of a backpack.
